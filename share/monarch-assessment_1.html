Why This Matters

Most inventory assessments are built on raw WMS exports — data that hasn't been validated, normalized, or cross-checked. That means the findings are only as good as the underlying data, which in Vendor B's case is unreliable 32% of the time. AI processing compresses what used to take days of manual cleanup into hours — and more importantly, it catches errors that a human analyst would likely miss. The 14 integrity issues flagged here would have distorted every metric in the report if left uncleaned.

Critical
Vendor B has no systematic SLA tracking — misses go undetected until client escalation
All three Vendor B sites lack automated SLA monitoring in their WMS. SLA compliance is tracked manually (or not at all) — meaning misses compound before anyone knows they're happening. This is the single largest P&L risk in the assessment.
Sites: B1, B2, B3

Fix: WMS SLA module + daily reporting
Timeline: 30 days

Memphis (B2) dock-to-stock approaching SLA breach — immediate staffing review needed
At 47 hours average, Site B2 is 1 hour from breaching the standard 48-hour dock-to-stock SLA. During peak days, it already exceeds 52 hours. Root cause is a combination of receiving staff turnover and no cross-training protocol.
Site: B2
Est. Exposure: $65K/yr
Fix: Cross-train 4 staff + receiving SOP refresh
Timeline: 2 weeks

Sites: B2, B3

High
Vendor A's Denver SOP model is replicable — and should be standardized across all sites
Site A1 (Denver) demonstrates best-practice inventory control processes that directly explain its 99.1% accuracy rate. This model is fully documented and transferable. Applying it to the four underperforming sites is the highest-ROI recommendation in this report.
